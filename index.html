The Information Asymmetry

Reading a food label shouldn’t require a biochemistry degree.

Front-of-pack claims are designed to market. Regulatory tables are designed to comply. Neither is designed for individual human clarity.

campaign

Deceptive Front-of-Pack Claims

Products proudly tout “Zero Added Sugar” or “Source of Fiber” while masking maltodextrin, high sodium, or saturated palm oils in the fine print.

Interactive Deception Check
Front Claim: “High Protein 15g • No Added Refined Sugar”
science

Cryptic Additives & E-Numbers

Codes like INS 500(ii), INS 450(i), and Acidity Regulator 330 disguise chemical preservation techniques that confuse consumers managing allergies or gut sensitivities.

INS 450(i) Disodium Diphosphate
INS 500(ii) Sodium Bicarbonate
person_off

One-Size-Fits-None Reference DV%

Daily percentage values reflect an arbitrary 2,000 kcal standard. A diabetic patient, a renal health tracker, and an endurance athlete require fundamentally different ratios.

Engineering Guardrails

Responsible AI & Data Integrity Principles

Foundational design standards preventing misleading claims and algorithmic biases.

not_interested

Missing ≠ Zero

If sugar or sodium values are absent from packaging data, NutriLabel flags them as “Unavailable” rather than assuming zero.

lock_clock

Strict Fact Grounding

The Grok API prompt template prohibits extrapolating values not explicitly present in the input JSON payload.

health_and_safety

Non-Clinical Disclaimer

Clear architectural boundary: educational decision-support only. Does not replace prescribed medical diets.

shield_person

On-Device Profile Caching

Personal health goals and allergen vulnerabilities remain cached securely in local client SQLite/Room storage.
